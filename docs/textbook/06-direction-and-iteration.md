# Chapter 6: Prompting, Direction, and Iteration

## Human Direction as Engineering Input

In AI-assisted development, user instructions function like evolving product requirements.

This is one of the most important skills students can learn now: AI systems are powerful, but literal. Better outcomes come from better direction.

## What Happened in This Project

The project evolved through clear direction changes:

- Start with a professional Next.js baseline
- Add chat behavior
- Replace form-based math with conversational MCP tools
- Enforce tool usage and remove unsupported claims
- Add real-LLM evals

This progression mirrors a broader shift discussed in AI industry conversations: we are moving from chat systems that mostly "talk" to systems that can "do" work through tools.

## Why This Is Relevant Right Now

Students are entering a job market where:

- AI can produce large amounts of output quickly
- Organizations increasingly value people who can define goals, evaluate results, and enforce quality
- "Looks productive" is not the same as "is correct" or "improves learning"

The goal of this course is not to train students to copy AI output. The goal is to train students to direct, verify, and improve AI-supported work.

## Why This Matters for Students

Students should learn to:

- Give precise constraints
- Detect misalignment quickly
- Iterate toward stronger architecture
- Require verification before accepting changes

## Working Principle: Do Not Outsource Thinking

Use AI to accelerate implementation and reduce repetitive work. Do not outsource core reasoning, taste, ethics, or accountability.

Students should preserve direct engagement with primary materials, first drafts, and design decisions.

## Direct Message to Students from GPT-5.3-Codex

I am GPT-5.3-Codex, and here is what I want you to practice in this course:

1. Be specific. If your request is vague, my output will drift.
1. Give constraints. Tell me what not to do, not just what to do.
1. Demand evidence. Ask for tests, evals, and failure cases.
1. Keep ownership. You are the engineer of record, not me.

If you use me well, I can help you move faster.
If you use me lazily, I can help you be wrong faster.

## Practice Rule

When behavior is critical, define acceptance criteria and verify with executable checks.
